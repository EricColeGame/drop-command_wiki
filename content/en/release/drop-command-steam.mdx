Drop Command is a Steam game from developer PillBugGames, released on 28 September 2026 (Steam app 1944880). Its pitch is mech combat at a grand scale: combined-arms battles where you pilot any unit under your command, drop into a fight alone or with a squad, and push through a battlefield that changes as debris and wreckage pile up. This article sticks to what the official store page actually states, flags what third-party guides claim, and is honest about what nobody has documented yet.

## What Drop Command Officially Advertises

According to the official Steam store page, Drop Command is built around **combined-arms combat at a grand scale**, with the player piloting any unit under their command. The advertised scope runs from small skirmishes up to epic battles. Two structural ideas stand out:

- **You drop into combat your way.** The store describes dropping in alone, leading a squad, or commanding an army.
- **The battlefield evolves.** Fully destructible debris and unit wreckage accumulate during a fight, changing the terrain you and your opponents are working with.

The game also advertises customisable and levellable pilots, with a choice between elite squads of veterans or disposable rookies you can rush at an opponent. On the hardware side, the store lists personalisable **Mechs, Tanks, VTOLs, and Battle Armour** — four broad unit categories rather than a detailed roster.

The page also mentions "building a stellar empire," which fits the strategic framing of the game but is not expanded on publicly.

## What the Store Page Does Not Document

This is where a lot of search traffic goes wrong. The official page does **not** publish:

## Piloting, Command, and Unit Choice

The headline mechanic — pilot any unit under your command — is a strong design signal even without numbers. In combined-arms games, this normally means the difference between a Mech, a Tank, a VTOL, and Battle Armour is *role*, not just durability. Mechs tend to anchor a fight and absorb attention. Tanks usually trade mobility for direct fire. VTOLs bring verticality and speed but are exposed. Battle Armour sits at the small, agile end.

Since the store confirms all four categories are personalisable, the practical question is not "which is best" — that depends on tuning nobody has published — but **which unit lets you influence the fight you are actually in**. If your side is losing the ground fight, a VTOL may let you reposition faster than a Mech ever could. If you are holding a line, a heavier platform usually buys your squad time.

## Rookies, Veterans, and Squad Building

The official description offers a genuine tension: build squads of veterans, or rush opponents with rookies. That framing suggests veterans carry experience and survivability, while rookies are cheaper or faster to field and therefore expendable.

General reasoning from the premise:

- **Rushing rookies** suits early pressure, wide maps, and opponents who need time to set up heavy units.
- **Veteran squads** suit long fights where individual units survive and compound their value.
- Because pilots are levellable, losing a pilot likely costs more than losing a chassis. That is a reasonable inference from "customisable and levellable pilots," not a documented rule.

No XP curve, level cap, or squad size limit is published, so any guide claiming exact numbers is guessing.

## Reading the Evolving Battlefield

| Advertised feature | Practical implication |
|---|---|
| Destructible debris | Cover may appear or vanish mid-fight |
| Unit wreckage | Battlefields get cluttered, blocking or channeling movement |
| Drop in alone, with a squad, or an army | Scale appears to be a player choice per engagement |

If wreckage accumulates, mobility and sightlines degrade over time. That favours players who keep a fast option in reserve rather than committing everything into one lane. It also means a battle you are winning early can still stall late.
