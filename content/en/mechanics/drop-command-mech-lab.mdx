## The "Pilot Any Unit" Idea and Why It Matters

The most structurally interesting claim here is that you pilot any unit under your command. In practice, that suggests a command layer sitting on top of a direct-control layer: you give orders, then take the controls of one asset when you want to influence a specific part of the fight.

This is a design pattern, not a documented mechanic with numbers attached. General reasoning from that premise suggests a few habits:

- Switching into a unit is a way to concentrate your attention, not a free power boost. The battle continues while you are inside one cockpit.
- Because the battlefield carries destructible debris and unit wreckage, terrain state is likely to change during a match, so a position that works early may not hold later.
- Combined arms implies that no single platform covers everything. The store page names Mechs, Tanks, VTOLs and Battle Armour, which suggests distinct roles rather than reskins.

Treat all three points as inference from the advertised concept. They are useful for planning, but they are not promises about balance, and the game's actual tuning should be checked in play.

## Solo Drops, Squads, and Army Command

The store page describes three broad modes of engagement: dropping alone, leading a squad, or commanding an army. The page also mentions building a stellar empire, though it does not explain what that means mechanically.

If you are deciding how to spend your first sessions, the natural reading is a scaling ladder. Dropping alone is the smallest unit of the game and the easiest place to learn a chassis without consequences for other units. A squad adds the question of what the veterans are for. An army adds the question of how many subordinates you can meaningfully direct before your attention is the bottleneck.

The pilot system reinforces this. Pilots are described as customisable and levelable, and you can field veteran formations or rush with rookies. A reasonable, non-data-backed approach is to treat veteran pilots as the assets you build around and rookies as the volume you are willing to lose. How large the gap between a veteran and a rookie actually is, and whether rookie losses carry any cost, is not documented on the store page. Verify it in-game.

## Prioritising What to Learn First

Given that no public source documents unit stats or build costs, the practical question is what to train first. Reasoning from a combined-arms premise, three skills transfer across almost any tuning:

1. Reading a battlefield that changes. Destructible debris and wreckage mean cover that existed at the start may be gone later. Learning to re-evaluate a position mid-fight matters more than memorising a spot.
2. Knowing when to take direct control. If you can pilot any unit, your attention is a resource. Spending it on the unit in the decisive fight is the obvious default.
3. Understanding your unit mix before the drop. Mechs, Tanks, VTOLs and Battle Armour are named as separate platform families, so a plan that ignores one category is a plan with a blind spot.

None of this guarantees results. It is a way to make decisions while the community's knowledge of the game is still thin.
