Drop Command is a large-scale mech combined-arms combat game from developer PillBugGames, released on Steam on 28 September 2026. Its central idea is simple to state and unusual in practice: instead of locking you to one hero machine, it lets you pilot any unit under your command. That single design decision reshapes almost every tactical question in the game, from how you open a fight to how you recover from a bad one.

This article explains what the game actually advertises, what the Steam listing leaves undocumented, and how to reason about battles, pilots and customisation without inventing numbers. You can check the official listing at [Drop Command on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1944880/) for anything that changes after release.

## The combined-arms premise and how to reason from it

Combined arms means different unit classes covering each other's weaknesses. Battle Armour, Tanks, VTOLs and Mechs suggest a familiar split: infantry-scale units that can occupy awkward ground, tracked vehicles with staying power, aircraft with speed and reach, and Mechs as the flexible heavy element. Because the game confirms you can pilot any of them, the interesting question is not which is strongest but which one you should personally be in at a given moment.

General reasoning that follows from the premise, rather than measured data:

| Situation | What the premise suggests you consider |
|---|---|
| Opening a fight | Start in the unit that answers the immediate threat, not your favourite |
| Losing a key asset | Switch into the unit holding the line rather than watching from a dying one |
| Broken terrain and debris | Slower, stable units may hold ground better than fast ones |
| Wide, open maps | Reach matters, which points toward fast or airborne options |
| Late, messy battles | Wreckage and debris change sightlines, so re-read the field |

The advertised destructible debris and unit wreckage matter here. If the environment changes as the fight goes on, cover that existed at minute one may not exist at minute ten, and wreckage can become either obstruction or protection. Treat the battlefield as a variable, not a backdrop.

## Pilots, squads and the rookie question

Two advertised systems interact: customisable, levelable pilots, and the choice between elite squads and disposable rookies. That combination creates a genuine tension. Veterans presumably carry experience and possibly skills you have invested in, while rookies are cheaper to lose and easier to replace. The store page does not say how pilots gain experience, what customisation options exist, or whether losses are permanent, so those specifics must be checked in-game.

As a general approach, the premise suggests keeping a veteran core for the fights that decide a campaign or a match, and using rookies where the expected loss rate is high. Whether that is efficient depends on costs and progression that are not documented publicly. A player can verify it by watching how pilot progression and replacement actually behave over a few battles.

## The "drop alone" option and why it exists

Drop Command lets you drop into combat alone, lead a squad, or command an army. These are three different jobs sharing one combat system. Dropping alone puts the emphasis entirely on your piloting and your unit choice, with no friendly units to absorb mistakes or create openings. Leading a squad asks you to direct a small number of units while still flying one of them. Commanding an army shifts the weight toward positioning and timing across many units, where your own machine is a piece of the whole.

Because you can pilot any unit under your command, the scale of the fight does not change whether you are personally in danger. It changes how much attention you can spare. A reasonable habit is to accept that at army scale you cannot micro everything, and to decide which units deserve your direct control and which should be left to hold a position.

## Customisation and practical starting advice

Mechs, Tanks, VTOLs and Battle Armour are all described as personalisable, and pilots are both customisable and levelable. No build costs, slot counts or modification lists are published, so any specific loadout advice would be invention rather than information.

What a new player can do without invented numbers:

- Play each of the four unit types at least once, since the game is built around piloting any unit under your command.
- Note how destructible debris and wreckage affect a map you have already fought on, because the battlefield is described as evolving.
- Experiment with squad composition using rookies first, keeping veterans for fights you expect to matter.
- Check the official Steam page and in-game tooltips for anything numeric rather than trusting a summary.

None of this guarantees success. It is reasoning from an advertised combined-arms premise, and the game's actual balance will only be visible once you are in it.
