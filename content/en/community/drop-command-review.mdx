Drop Command is a mech combat game built around a simple but ambitious idea: you are not locked into one vehicle. According to the official [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1944880/), PillBugGames released the game on 28 September 2026, and its pitch is "mech combat at a grand scale." You pilot any unit under your command, fight everything from small skirmishes to epic battles, and either drop in alone or lead a squad or an army. That flexibility is the whole point of the game, and it changes how you should think about every match.

This article stays close to what the developer actually advertises. Where something is undocumented, it says so plainly. Where advice is just reasoning from the game's premise, it is labeled as such.

## What Drop Command actually is

The verified official description frames Drop Command as combined-arms combat where you control any unit in your force. The advertised pillars are:

- Mech combat at a grand scale
- Piloting any unit under your command
- Fights ranging from small skirmishes to epic battles
- Dropping in alone, with a squad, or commanding an army
- An evolving battlefield with fully destructible debris and unit wreckage
- Customizable and levelable pilots
- Elite squads of veterans or disposable rookies
- Personalizable Mechs, Tanks, VTOLs, and Battle Armour

That list is the developer's own summary. It is worth reading carefully, because it tells you what kind of decisions the game rewards: force composition, pilot management, and battlefield awareness rather than a single fixed loadout.

## The combined-arms premise, and what it implies

"Combined arms" means different unit types are meant to cover each other's weaknesses. The store page names four chassis families: Mechs, Tanks, VTOLs, and Battle Armour. It does not publish unit stats, damage tables, build costs, or a tech tree. Any guide claiming exact numbers is going beyond what the developer has documented.

What you can reasonably infer from the premise, without inventing data:

- If you can pilot any unit, the value of a unit type depends on the situation, not on a universal tier list.
- VTOLs suggest vertical mobility and air pressure; Tanks suggest ground presence and staying power; Battle Armour suggests small, agile infantry-scale action; Mechs suggest a flexible middle ground. These are reasonable readings of the names, not confirmed balance facts.
- Because the battlefield has destructible debris and wreckage, cover is probably temporary. Do not treat any piece of terrain as permanent.

Treat all of the above as general reasoning. Verify it in-game before you build a strategy around it.

## Pilots: veterans versus rookies

The official page advertises "customizable and levelable pilots" and a choice between "elite squads" and "disposable rookies." This is a resource decision as much as a tactics decision.

A practical way to think about it, again as reasoning rather than measured advice:

| Approach | Likely trade-off | When it may fit |
|---|---|---|
| Veteran squad | Higher investment, more to lose | Longer engagements, objectives you must hold |
| Rookie rush | Cheaper bodies, faster to field | Early pressure, probing, absorbing risk |

The page does not state how pilot leveling works, what a level grants, or whether veterans can be lost permanently. Check the in-game pilot menu and any official patch notes for the current rules.

## Dropping in: solo, squad, or army

Drop Command explicitly supports three scales of play: alone, leading a squad, or commanding an army. The official description does not explain how command is issued, whether you can hot-swap between units mid-match, or how large an "army" is.

If you are learning the game, dropping in alone is the cleanest way to understand a single chassis without also managing subordinates. Once you are comfortable, adding a squad introduces positioning questions: where do your units stand relative to yours, and what happens when one dies? Larger battles multiply those questions. The developer has not documented command hotkeys or control schemes, so confirm them in the options menu rather than trusting a third-party list.

## Customization and the evolving battlefield

The advertised customization covers Mechs, Tanks, VTOLs, and Battle Armour, plus pilots. No build costs, slot counts, or part names are published. Third-party guides may describe loadouts they have used, but those are observations from players, not developer documentation, and they can go stale after a patch.

The "evolving battlefield" claim matters tactically. Fully destructible debris and unit wreckage mean the map you scouted at the start is not the map you fight in later. Rotate your approach routes, and expect wreckage to create new sightlines and new obstacles.
