## Understanding the Combined-Arms Premise

Combined arms traditionally means different unit types cover each other's weaknesses. The store page names four chassis families: Mech, Tank, VTOL, and Battle Armour. It does not publish unit stats, damage tables, or build costs, so any claim about which chassis is "best" would be guesswork.

What the verified description does support is that you can pilot any unit under your command. That single sentence has a large practical consequence: you are not locked into one role for an entire battle. A sensible general approach is to treat your piloting choice as a response to the situation rather than a permanent identity. If a fight stalls in one dimension, switching the unit you personally control may change what you can influence.

## Solo Drops, Squads, and Army Command

The store page presents three broad modes of engagement: dropping alone, leading a squad, or commanding an army. These are not just difficulty settings; they change what your attention is for.

When you drop alone, all decisions are yours, and there is no one else to absorb mistakes. When you lead a squad, you gain the ability to be in one place while your squad does something elsewhere. When you command an army, the volume of simultaneous events likely exceeds what one person can watch, which makes prioritisation the core skill.

The verified description does not explain the specific command interface, so how orders are issued is something you will need to learn in-game. A reasonable way to verify the details is to check the official store page media and any developer posts, and then confirm in play.

## Veterans, Rookies, and Pilot Progression

Two advertised systems work together here: customisable and levelable pilots, plus the choice between elite squads and disposable rookies. The wording implies a trade-off between quality and quantity or replaceability, but the store page does not publish experience curves, level caps, or stat growth.

Because pilots can be customised and level up, it is reasonable to assume that investment in a pilot compounds over time. From that premise, a common pattern in games with this structure is to protect experienced pilots and use less-invested units for riskier tasks. Treat that as reasoning, not a documented rule.

## An Evolving Battlefield

Fully destructible debris and unit wreckage are explicitly advertised. The interesting word is "evolving." If wreckage persists, then the map you fight on late in a battle is not the map you started on. Practically, that suggests two habits worth forming:

1. Re-read the terrain before committing to a route, rather than memorising an opening plan.
2. Treat destroyed units as information about where fighting has concentrated.

How much wreckage actually persists, and whether it meaningfully blocks movement or line of sight, is not documented on the store page. You can verify it directly by playing a longer battle and observing whether the battlefield layout changes.
