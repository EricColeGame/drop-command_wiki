## What Drop Command Actually Advertises

The store description is the authority here. It states that Drop Command is mech combat at a grand scale with combined-arms combat, and that you pilot any unit under your command. It lists these advertised features:

- Combat ranging from small skirmishes to large battles.
- An evolving battlefield with fully destructible debris and unit wreckage.
- Dropping into combat alone, leading a squad, or commanding an army.
- Customisable and levelable pilots.
- Building squads of elite veterans or rushing opponents with disposable rookies.
- Personalisable Mechs, Tanks, VTOLs, and Battle Armour.

That is the whole advertised feature set. Notably, the store page does not publish unit statistics, damage tables, build costs, tech trees, faction lists, or any code system. If you see a guide claiming exact numbers, treat it as a third-party observation rather than developer documentation.

## Piloting Any Unit Changes How You Think

Most mech games ask you to master one machine. Drop Command's advertised hook is that the machine is a decision. In practice, that reframes a lot of moment-to-moment choices. If you can jump into a tank when the ground fight stalls, or a VTOL when you need reach, your job is less "get good at one chassis" and more "read the battle and pick the tool."

This is where combined arms matters. A force of a single unit type tends to have one obvious weakness. Mixing Mechs, Tanks, VTOLs, and Battle Armour spreads that weakness across the field. Because the store page also advertises destructible debris and wreckage as an evolving battlefield, cover and terrain are likely to shift as a fight drags on — general reasoning from the "evolving battlefield" line, not measured data.

## Command Scale: Alone, Squad, or Army

The advertised command options are alone, a squad, or an army. That is a wide range, and it suggests the game supports very different playstyles rather than one mandatory structure.

- **Alone.** Fewer moving parts, more personal control. Best when you want to feel every input matter.
- **Squad.** A middle ground where you can lead a handful of units without managing a full battle line.
- **Army.** The largest scale, where positioning and unit mix matter more than any single engagement.

These are framings of the advertised options, not measured performance claims. How the game actually balances each scale is something only play will show.

## Veterans, Rookies, and Pilot Progression

Two advertised systems sit close together: customisable and levelable pilots, and the choice between elite squads and disposable rookies. The implied tension is resource management. Veterans presumably carry accumulated experience you would rather not lose. Rookies are cheaper to field and easier to replace, but likely less capable.

The "rush opponents with rookies" line suggests a legitimate aggressive strategy exists in the design, not just a fallback. If you like attrition play, that is the advertised lane for it. If you prefer preserving a core of experienced pilots, the leveling system is the advertised lane for that. No experience curves, level caps, or stat growth values are documented, so verify those in game.
